Most “free online PDF redactor” sites ask you to upload the exact document you’re trying to protect. DocBurn does the whole job inside your browser tab: pdf.js renders the pages locally, you draw the redactions, and the new PDF is built on your device. Nothing is sent to a server.

Steps (about a minute)

  1. Open DocBurn. Go to the DocBurn app in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari). The page loads its scripts once; your PDF is never transmitted.
  2. Drop your PDF. Drag the file onto the page or use the file picker. Pages render locally in the tab.
  3. Draw black boxes. Drag rectangles over names, account numbers, SSNs, emails, signatures — anything that must go.
  4. Export. DocBurn rasterizes each page with the black burned into the pixels and saves a new PDF. There is no hidden text layer left under the boxes.
  5. Check the result. Open the exported PDF and try to select or search the redacted words. They aren’t there to find.

Why “no upload” matters for redaction

If a document needs redacting, it already contains something you don’t want copied. Uploading it to a third-party converter means trusting that service’s storage, retention, logs, and staff with the unredacted original. A client-side tool removes that step entirely: the file goes from your disk into your browser’s memory and back to your disk.

How to confirm a tool really stays local

  • Open your browser’s DevTools → Network tab, then load a PDF. A local tool shows no request carrying your file.
  • After the page has loaded, turn off Wi-Fi and try it. Client-side processing keeps working.
  • DocBurn is a static site on GitHub Pages — there is no backend to receive files.

Trade-off to know about

Because DocBurn burns redactions into page images, the exported PDF is image-based: the remaining text isn’t selectable or searchable either. For sharing a redacted copy, that’s usually the point. Keep your original for your own records.
