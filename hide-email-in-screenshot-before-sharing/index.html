How to hide an email address in a screenshot before sharing

Email addresses show up everywhere in screenshots: inbox lists, account pages, CC lines, browser profile menus, autocomplete dropdowns. Here’s a quick routine to hide them properly before a screenshot goes into Slack, a bug report, or a post.

The 60-second routine

  1. Scan the whole image. Look beyond the obvious field: tab titles, profile avatars/menus, To/CC/BCC lines, signatures, notification toasts, and URL bars (some contain emails or tokens).
  2. Open BlotOut. Drop the screenshot into BlotOut in your browser. It isn’t uploaded.
  3. Box every address. Drag solid black boxes over each email. Optional auto-detect can suggest email-shaped text for you to accept.
  4. Export and re-check. Export the PNG, open it at 100% zoom, and confirm nothing is readable at the edges of each box.
  5. Share the export, not the original. Attach the exported file. Delete or keep the original privately.

Why not just blur or crop?

  • Blur/pixelate can leave enough information to guess or reconstruct text, especially short predictable strings like email addresses. Solid fill leaves nothing.
  • Cropping is great when the email is at an edge — but check the crop was actually applied to the exported file, not stored as an editable crop.
  • Semi-transparent markers (highlighter tools) can often be “seen through” by adjusting brightness/contrast.

Common places emails hide in screenshots

  • Top-right account menus in Google, Microsoft, and SaaS dashboards
  • Calendar invites and attendee lists
  • Git commit author lines and CI logs
  • Password manager and autofill popups
